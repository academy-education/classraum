## What this is

You are reading 20 multiple-choice questions from our TOEFL practice
bank and answering two questions about each one. It takes about 20
minutes.

Someone has already read these same 20 items. You are reading them
independently so we can compare. **That comparison is the whole point,
so please do not ask anyone what they answered, and do not look at any
existing results before you finish.**

There is no score for you here. A low number is not a bad performance —
it is information about the questions, which is what we are measuring.

## Each item has two steps

### Step 1 — blind

You see **four options and nothing else**. No reading passage, no
audio, no question stem. The screen says *"Blind — no stimulus. Which is
the intended answer?"*

Pick the option you think was meant to be correct.

You are not being asked to know the answer — you cannot, because the
source is hidden. You are being asked whether the options alone give it
away. So:

- If something about the options points to one of them, pick it.
- If nothing does, press **"Can't tell — a real answer, not a skip"**.
  That is a genuine answer and is recorded as one. **Please use it.**
  Guessing when you have nothing to go on is the single thing that would
  most distort this measurement, and there is no penalty for saying you
  cannot tell.

### Step 2 — revealed

Now you see the passage, the question and the real answer. Two
judgements:

**Is the key the only defensible answer?**

| Choose | When |
|---|---|
| Only defensible answer | The marked answer is right and nothing else works |
| Another is also defensible | A second option could be argued for by a reasonable candidate |
| No unique answer / key is wrong | Two or more work equally well, or the marked answer is simply wrong |

Be strict on the middle option. "Defensible" means a careful test-taker
could argue for it, not that it is equally good.

**Does it read like a real exam question?**

*Authentic*, or *Authored to a template* if it feels machine-made or
formulaic.

There is a free-text note box. Use it whenever something is off — an
awkward phrase, an option that gives the game away, anything you would
flag if you were proofing the test. Past sittings found real defects
that no automated check had caught, and those all came from the notes.

## Please finish in one go

The 20 items are drawn and recorded before you see anything, so items
you skip still count in the total. Stopping halfway leaves a partial
sitting that cannot be compared against the first one.

When the last item is submitted you are done — nothing to send, it
saves as you go.
