- `map-pilot-mech.batch.json`: 6 Capitalization + 6 Spelling, RIT 180-239 targets

## Options-only screen (36-item render, three samples of one Claude solver)

The render holds 24 candidates plus 12 live easy ISEE verbal items as control
(6 synonym, 6 sentence completion). Keys are dealt flat **per arm**: 3/3/3/3
each, best-fixed-letter 25.0%.

The first render dealt each arm 5/4/2/1. That is flat over the file but 41.7%
inside an arm. It was re-dealt before any solver saw it.

| stratum | n | mean of 3 samples | own control | unanimous on key |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mechanics** (cap + spelling) | 12 | **100.0%** | 25.0% | 12/12 |
| **R/LU** | 12 | 63.9% | 25.0% | 7/12 |
| live ISEE easy verbal (control) | 12 | 61.1% | 25.0% | 5/12 |

**These numbers are not pooled, and the pooled rate decides nothing.**

**Mechanics at 100% is the construct, not a defect.** Four spellings of one
word, or four versions of one sentence that differ only in case, contain the
entire item. The pre-registration declared this stratum uninformative before
it ran, for the same reason SAT SEC is exempt from "passage needed".

**What the arm-level R/LU number hid.** At the arm level R/LU sits 2.8 points
from the control and passes. Inside the arm it splits completely by item type:

| sub-stratum | items | blind |
|---|---|---|
| passage comprehension: theme, summary, central idea, point of view, purpose (RLU-01..05) | 5 | **15/15, all unanimous** |
| writing (RLU-08 transition, RLU-09 word choice) | 2 | 5/6 |
| grammar (RLU-10, -11, -12) | 3 | 3/9 (RLU-11 3/3 is the fragment/run-on construct) |
| vocabulary in context (RLU-06, -07) | 2 | **0/6** |

The control is vocabulary-shaped, so it is matched to the stratum that came in
clean and not to the one that leaked. **Every passage-comprehension item was
solved without its passage.** Per the CLAUDE.md rule, unanimity alone drops
nothing. The with-source half supplies the corroboration: both graders,
independently, named a free elimination on all five, for example:

- an absolute ("always")
- a narrow detail offered as a central idea
- a summary option that adds invented details

This is the CLAUDE.md "options differ along the axis the stem names" leak,
reproduced at grade 5. It is not cause to drop these five, since they cleared
exclusivity, but it is the main thing a second batch must change.

**What the solvers said.** Samples A and B reported their heuristics, and C's picks track theirs (36/36 on Mechanics, the same 7 R/LU items unanimous). Both reports named the same mechanism:

- "only one option is correct", on all 12 Mechanics items plus RLU-11
- "the one main-idea/theme-shaped option", on the passage items
- "no signal / guess", on vocabulary and most grammar

## With-source graders (two fresh Claude graders, key unmarked)

**18 of 24 reach pilot-pass.** Six are held:

- **RLU-12:** both graders name B as a second defensible answer. The "was"
  option agrees in number and is excluded only by tense, so the item tests
  tense, not agreement. **Exclusivity failure.**
- **RLU-09, MECH-07, MECH-08:** both graders rate these too easy for the
  stated grade:
  - "creaked" for stairs is grade 3-4 word choice, labelled grade 7
  - "because" is a grade 1-2 sight word
  - "swimming" doubling is grade 2-3
- **MECH-05, MECH-09:** one grader each rates these too easy.

**The centroid tell, found by script and named by both graders.**

- **Measured:** in 10 of 11 scorable Mechanics items, the key is the unique
  "consensus" option. That covers all 6 capitalization items and 4 of 5
  correct-polarity spelling items. Each distractor deviates from the key at
  exactly one place, so a per-token majority vote finds the key with no
  knowledge of the rule (`map-pilot-posthoc.mjs` P2, break-tested; chance
  expectation is about 2.8 of 11). This is arithmetic, so it was checked
  over the whole population, not sampled.
- **Grader B** was not prompted with the idea and called it "feature-majority
  vote" on 10 items.
- **Grader A's prompt** gave "the key being the option the others all agree
  with" as an example of a free elimination. That primed it, so its
  consensus/majority mentions are not independent evidence.

**Unlike the options-only 100%, this one is a real defect.** A test-wise
student without the rule can use it. It was not a pre-registered check, so it
does not change the verdict above. It is recorded as the second thing batch 2
must change.

**Dead distractors.** Both graders found options no real student would pick:

- "swimmming" with a triple m
- "buzzed"/"splashed" for stairs
- "lena" lowercase
- "Town" capitalized

A dead distractor turns a four-option item into a two-option one and pulls
its difficulty below the target band. This is why every band miss went the
same way.

**Band.** Grader A rates 10/24 "easier" than target, grader B 7/24; nobody
rated anything "harder". The bar (at least 50% by both) is not triggered.
Still, a 17-of-48 call rate with zero in the other direction says the
authors' sense of "RIT 220-239" is generous. The Mechanics upper bands are
the weakest:

- MECH-05, a split quotation targeted at 220-229, is estimated at about 200-210.
- MECH-06 at 230-239 is called high by both graders.

Per MAP-RESEARCH §10, no band label is validated by this. Graders reading
norms tables are not response data.

**Grade fit.** 20/24 "fits" from each grader. The 4 too-easy calls
concentrate in exactly the items above.

## What a second batch should change

1. **Mechanics: break the centroid.** Distractors must not all be one edit
   from the key. Mix one-error and two-error distractors, and put the key
   off-consensus in at least half of items, for example two distractors that
   share an error the key lacks. Make P2 a pre-registered gate: the key may be
   the unique centroid in at most about 30% of items.
2. **Mechanics: no dead distractors.** Every distractor must be an error real
   students make at that grade. That means no triple letters, no lowercase
   proper names in sentences above grade 4, and no capitalized random common
   nouns. Cap items should spend all three distractors on the rule being
   tested.
3. **Reading: the comprehension items leak by option shape.** Theme, summary
   and central-idea options must be the same KIND of statement, varying only
   in what the passage decides:
   - four passage-specific claims, not one apt claim beside a generic moral,
     an absolute and a narrow detail
   - summaries that all have the same scope and none that invent details
   This is the axis-alignment rule from CLAUDE.md, now shown at grade 5.
4. **Calibrate difficulty by word and rule, not by the band label.** Floor the
   spelling vocabulary at grade level: no grade-1-3 sight words labelled
   grade 5+. Check each target band against a grade-level word list before
   authoring. The 180-189 band for grade 5 is legitimately easy, but
   "because" is below even that.
5. **Grammar: one construct per item.** RLU-12 failed because tense and
   agreement both varied. Hold every non-tested feature fixed across the four
   options.
6. **Keep:** the per-arm flat deal, the E1 "not measured" handling, the
   vocabulary items (0/6 blind, clean with source), and authors writing only
   from public strand names. The next batch should stay small (about 24) and
   be attacked before a third is commissioned. A human sitting is still the
   only verdict on a verbal cohort, and none exists for MAP.
