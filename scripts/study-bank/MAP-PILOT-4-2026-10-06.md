## Fix 1 (dead distractors): what the teacher gate did

- Round 1: the teacher rater said **no** to 11 of 72 distractors on 10
  items, 6 of them vocabulary. Mechanics got 30/30 yes.
- The reviser rewrote all 11. The re-rate (a fresh teacher agent) still said
  no to **4 of the 11 rewrites**: LU-13 "excited", LU-19 "describes Room 12",
  LU-21 "his", LU-22 "carried, dripping…, the pizza". It also said no to 3
  options the first rater had passed: LU-11 "taste stale", LU-14 "proud",
  LU-16 "squeak loudly".
- **The graders' dead-by-both list was exactly three of those seven:** LU-21
  "his", LU-22 D and LU-11 "taste stale". Every dead-by-both option had been
  flagged by a teacher rater, and the gate's
  one-rewrite-then-go-as-is rule let all three through.
- So the teacher rater is predictive. Two instances disagree with each other
  (3 options were yes then no), so one pass misses some, and an
  unrepairable rewrite should have dropped the item rather than sent it
  on. **Next time: two independent teacher raters, union their no's, and
  drop any item whose rewrite is still rated no.**

## Fix 1's cost: the vocabulary options now leak and the items are easy

Options-only, three samples, stems withheld:

| usage | 8 | 66.7% | 25.0% | 5/8 | 33.3% |

| control R | 8 | 33.3% | 25.0% | 1/8 | 25.0% |
| control V | 12 | 52.8% | 25.0% | 4/12 | 58.3% |

**SCREEN FLAG: vocabulary is 36 points above control V** (the prereg's flag
is 20). The prereg predicted a rise as the cost of fix 1. It came in far
larger than that. The solvers' notes give the mechanism:
- "run low is the most general supply phrase"
- "mixed feelings is the nuanced odd-one-out"
- "doubtful is the only negative-skeptical option"

Under the new rule the distractors are words that fit the *situation*
("grow warm", "leak out", "taste stale"). The key is the only one that reads
as a **dictionary gloss of an unseen tier-2 word**. That is CLAUDE.md's
axis rule exactly: the options differ along the axis the stem names (which
of these is a definition?).

The with-source graders found the same mechanism from the other side:
- the context decides the meaning too directly (LU-11, 13, 16, all too_easy)
- grader A named LU-15's key as the structural odd one out

**The two vocabulary rules now measured each fail on one side:**
- **"genuine meanings of the word"** (batch 3): clean blind, 22.2%, but some
  options are dead.
- **"real words that fit the sentence's surface"** (batch 4): fewer dead
  options, but it leaks (88.9%) and plays easy.

Usage at 66.7% is mostly the construct: LU-18 has only one standard
reflexive, LU-22 places the modifier, and LU-20's "Neither … or" rewrite
announces the pair. One more is a cross-item prior: LU-23 "As a result" was
picked as the "most common key" by all three samples.

The controls moved a little from batches 2-3: R 25.0 → 33.3, V 58.3 → 52.8.
Both are inside the range where the screen is valid.

## Fix 2 (calibration): no movement

- `easier` was 12/48, against 11 in batch 3. Every `ccss` code matched its
  band's grade (0 mismatches), so the anchoring rule was followed. Following
  it did not make the items harder.
- **The misses moved up a band:** 7 of the 12 `easier` calls are at 190-199
  (vocabulary, the pronoun item, the transition) and 4 at 200-209. Only 1 is
  at 180-189.
- The hard-end grade-5 list did lift the lowest band. Graders rated two
  180-189 Mechanics items *harder* (LU-01 north/Midwest at 190-199 from
  both). The lowest band's 4 holds are now a key error, a style dispute, a
  second answer and one easy vocabulary item, not calibration.
- So "one band harder" works where the difficulty sits in a rule
  (Mechanics, grammar). It fails where it sits in a context clue
  (vocabulary, precise-word Writing). There the author keeps writing a clue
  that decides the answer.
