## atv2-b2-p1 (Materials Science, self-healing coating / Darex / Marek group)

**REFUTABILITY:** none decisive. Self-healing coatings are a real field, but the
coating, Darex, and the Marek group are fictional and no pivot is
outcome-identified by the real literature. Two priors, recorded but not
refutability: (a) q3 — "healing fades after a few repair cycles" is
textbook-expected for capsule-based systems, so a materials-savvy solver leans
q3 s1 ("convinced, several labs have now observed the same fading"); but the
fictional coating's mechanism is unspecified (intrinsic/vascular systems heal
repeatedly), so no setting is world-false. (b) q4 s3 "Fasteners used on
aircraft" is the sibling a solver ranks last for a FIRST large-scale use
(aviation certification conservatism) — the mechanistically-strange-sibling
shape, milder than pilot p4/q2 s1. Advisory only.

**ASYMMETRY:** q3 is a good mixed slate (endorse / rig-fault / reinterpret /
walked-back) with no lone hedged survivor — s3's "real but caused by moisture"
is a committed reinterpretation, not a hedge. q4's four settings are admirably
flat (four bare applications, same length and register).

**ORTHOGONALITY:** checked all six pairs. One transcript-authoring tension, not
a leak: q3 s3 (fading is real and moisture-caused) alongside q4 s2 ("interiors
of water pipelines") or s4 ("ship hulls below the waterline") asks the lecture
to assert moisture degrades the healing AND that the first big use is
permanently wet — writable (the professor can note the tension or scope the
moisture claim to cyclic wet/dry), but the transcript author must do the work
explicitly if those combinations are drawn. No pair forces or forbids another
pivot's setting.

**Verdict: CLEAN** (advisories: q4 s3 plausibility rank; q3 s3 × q4 s2/s4
transcript note).

## atv2-b2-p7 (Soil Science, Ansdell plots) — the batch's paradigm problem

**REFUTABILITY — severe on one pivot:** the lecture is a transparent clone of
Rothamsted (century-old fertilized/unfertilized plots, an archive of soil
samples compared with fresh soil, a founder's weather record, the
does-it-generalize-to-working-farms debate — that is the Sample Archive and
Broadbalk, recognizably). Recognition then decides q1: the canonical,
endlessly-reproduced Rothamsted result is that soil carbon on the long-term
arable plots is remarkably constant — **q1 s3, "It has scarcely changed in a
hundred years," is the textbook-true answer once the clone is recognized**,
and the siblings contradict the canonical curves (s1's rise on both, s2's
fall despite improved yields, s4's mid-century peak are each wrong AT
ROTHAMSTED). This is the dodo–tambalacoque / TMR shape the pilot killed: one
famous fact plus three settings a soils undergraduate can rank. The other
pivots survive recognition — the real institution has at various times done
everything in q3's slate (new trials, modernized treatments, strict
continuity), q2's Halvorsen/Iwu debate is fictional with no real-world winner,
and q4's diary functions are not decided by the paradigm.

**ASYMMETRY:** q2 is a well-built four-way verdict (with H / with I / with
neither / H's conclusion but not his reasoning) — no hedged survivor. One
exclusivity note: q2 s1 and s4 both side with Halvorsen's conclusion; the
transcript must kill the unchosen one explicitly (deny the commercial-farm
replications when s4 is drawn, and vice versa). Writable; note for the
kill-quote author.

**ORTHOGONALITY:** all pairs pass; no setting confirms or forbids a sibling's.

**Verdict: NEEDS REWRITE — p7/q1 (pivot-level).** Minimum fix: break the
outcome-identifiability, not just the names — either de-clone the design
enough that the canonical stable-carbon result no longer applies (a different
measured quantity, a site history that blocks the Broadbalk mapping), or
rebuild the slate so the recognizer's textbook answer is not among the
settings in identifiable form. Advisory: re-flavor the Rothamsted tells
(sample archive + weather diary + working-farms debate together are the
fingerprint) while q1 is being fixed.
