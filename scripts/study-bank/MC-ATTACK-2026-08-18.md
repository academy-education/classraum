# MC blind attack, all live TOEFL MC types except Choose a Response — 2026-08-18

Fresh per-type measurement of the 2026-07-31 finding ("all 5 MC types
93-100% solvable without audio/passage"), after the recent bank changes
and the cr-v7 ship. Choose a Response (cr-v7) deliberately NOT touched.

**Method** (the established one, attack-cohort.mjs render):

- Enumerated live (`archived=false`) TOEFL items per domain; paged past
  the PostgREST 1000-row cap and verified every paged read against a
  `count(*) exact` head query (mcatk-prepare.mjs refuses on mismatch).
- Populations: Academic Passage 434, Academic Talk 275, Conversation
  193 → stratified samples of 120 (cohort × difficulty, proportional,
  largest-remainder; seed `mcatk-2026-08-18:<slug>`, sample lists in
  `mcatk-<slug>.sample.json`). Daily Life 133 and Announcement 121 are
  ≤150 → whole population attacked.
- Blind render: stem (item prompt/question) + options only, per-item
  re-letter with the seeded shuffle — identical to
  conversation-2026-08-15.blind.json / attack-cohort.mjs. Passage /
  audio / transcript never crossed the line. Files
  `mcatk-<slug>.blind.json` / `.key.json`.
- 3 independent Claude solver subagents per type, each given ONLY its
  blind file, forced-choice on every item, one-line reason each. Raw
  outputs saved as `mcatk-<slug>.solver-{a,b,c}.json`.
- Scored by `mcatk-score.mjs`. **Scorer break-tested first**: solver-a
  of Academic Passage against a rotated key scored 28.3% (shift 7) and
  23.3% (shift 1) vs its 100.0% on the true key — the scorer reads
  content, not position.
- READ-ONLY throughout: nothing inserted into study_item_attacks or any
  table (this run is files-only by instruction; it will NOT appear in
  `attack-cohort.mjs report` coverage).

## Per-type detail

### Academic Passage — 120/434, blind 100.0%, control 30.0%, margin +70.0
Per cohort: harvest-v1 100.0% (100 items), orphan-v1 100.0% (20).
Every solver solved every item. These function as general-knowledge
questions: the key is the true fact and the distractors are false
facts, so no passage is needed.

Tells (solver keywords across 360 rationales: absolutes 88, hedged 50,
world knowledge 37). Verbatim solver rationales:
- "Hedged 'valued both X and Y'; others use absolutes
  'only/entirely/indifferent'."
- "Only balanced summary; B/C/D are too negative, narrow, or absolute."
- "Marketing is the application example; A/D are absurd."
- World knowledge decides vocabulary items outright: "symbiosis = a
  mutually beneficial relationship" is dictionary-true without the
  passage.

### Academic Talk — 120/275, blind 99.2%, control 29.2%, margin +70.0
Per cohort: v3-claude 100.0% (69), harvest-v1 100.0% (23), talk-c2
100.0% (8), talk-c1 96.7% (10), orphan-v1 93.3% (10). No cohort is
clean — the newest (v3-claude) is the most solvable.

Tells (world knowledge 49, hedged 38, absolutes 31): keys are the
textbook-true mechanism ("harvesting plants that retained their seeds
longer"); distractors are either false to domain knowledge or
anachronistic ("engineering the genes of seeds in modern
laboratories"). Verbatim solver rationales:
- "Syntrophy/exchange version is the hedged, nuanced survivor; A/B/D
  are stated too absolutely."
- "'This isn't the only change' standardly signals more effects to
  come."

### Daily Life — 133/133 (whole population), blind 95.5%, control 29.3%, margin +66.2
Single cohort (harvest-v1, all "hard"). 127/133 solved by all three
solvers — identical per-solver scores (127/133 each).

Tells (hedged 63, absolutes 29, world 26): distractors are often
cartoonishly imprudent — "Ignore their advisor's advice", "Register
without planning", "Wait until the last minute" — so the one
responsible-adult option is the key without any source text. Verbatim
solver rationales: "Only sensible advice; others are absurd
anti-advice"; "Waitlist is the standard consequence of a full class";
"Hedged inference; C/D are absolute, A over-restrictive".

### Conversation — 120/193, blind 93.1%, control 30.0%, margin +63.1
Per cohort: v3-claude 94.5% (55), harvest-v1 91.9% (41), orphan-v1
91.7% (24).

Tells (specificity 54, world 25): the key is the pragmatically natural
conversational move; distractors are moves no cooperative speaker would
make. Stems' bracketed context tags ([Conversation — Roommates]) plus
the question often reconstruct the dialogue. Verbatim solver
rationales:
- "Specific procedural detail (online form by 5 p.m.) is the classic
  key; fee/call/bring-everyone are implausible."
- "Duties-vs-results is the standard resume critique and pairs with
  item 91's numbers follow-up" — cross-item sibling leakage appears
  here too, not only in Announcement.

### Announcement — 121/121 (whole population), blind 93.9%, control 32.2%, margin +61.7
Per cohort: v3-claude 95.8% (80), harvest-v1 92.4% (22), orphan-v1
87.7% (19).

Tells — solver C's pattern report, verbatim highlights:
1. **Cross-item leakage, "the biggest tell by far"**: "Stems and
   distractors from sibling items about the same announcement reveal
   each other's answers … item 39's answer 'welcome dinner was moved
   from Friday to Thursday' appears verbatim as distractors in items
   76C and 89D … Roughly 30-40 items were solvable by cross-referencing
   alone." Multiple questions per announcement ship into the bank and
   answer one another.
2. "Extremes lose, hedged/compound middles win" — "all/only/never/
   canceled entirely" marks distractors.
3. "The key is the thing a real office would actually say" — campus-
   policy world knowledge ("official emergency text alert system" not
   social media).
4. Purpose questions: "the umbrella option that subsumes the detail
   stems" wins.

Note the cross-item leakage tell means the whole-population blind file
is itself an amplifier for Announcement (and plausibly Conversation /
Daily Life): a student sees far fewer sibling items in one sitting, so
the per-item tells (2)–(4) are the student-relevant ones; the leakage
is a bank-structure defect that also matters for any full-form draw
that seats siblings together.

## Recommendation — rebuild first

**Academic Talk** first, with **Academic Passage** as the close second
(or jointly, since the defect is identical):

- Both sit at +70.0 margin, the ceiling of this measurement, and
  Academic Talk is at 99–100% across EVERY cohort including v3-claude,
  the most recently authored — so re-authoring under the current
  pipeline reproduces the defect (same lesson as cr-v1→v6).
- The defect class is the worst kind: the key is the TRUE FACT and the
  distractors are false facts, so the item measures world knowledge,
  not listening/reading. That cannot be patched per-item; it needs the
  cr-v7 lesson — a construction in which the correct answer is not
  derivable from truth alone (e.g. lecture states a counter-intuitive /
  fictionalized position, or key selected among all-true options by
  what was actually said).
- Why Academic Talk over Academic Passage given the tie: (a) it is a
  Listening type, where "solvable with the audio withheld" most
  directly voids the construct; (b) 275 items vs 434 makes the rebuild
  tractable sooner; (c) Academic Passage at least retains reading-
  comprehension value for vocabulary-in-context items, while a talk
  solvable without the talk measures nothing the section claims to.
- Announcement, despite ranking last by margin, carries a distinct
  structural defect (sibling-item leakage) that is cheap to mitigate
  independently of authoring: never seat two questions from the same
  announcement in one form, and dedupe verbatim option/answer echoes
  across siblings.

Caveat per the B4 demotion: these are model-side numbers; the human
sitting remains the verdict instrument. But at +61 to +70 with three
solvers agreeing on 110–127 items per type, these are the same
magnitudes that were human-confirmed broken on Choose a Response
(+45), and far beyond the margins a human later contradicted (e.g.
Announcement's older 100% vs 15% human — the per-item tells here are
weaker than the raw % suggests, which is consistent with that history;
the leakage tell, however, a human WOULD be able to use in a full
sitting).
