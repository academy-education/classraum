Hi — this is a 20-minute job, twice if you have the time, and it is the
single most useful thing anyone can do on the question bank right now.

## What this is

You will read questions from our TOEFL and SAT practice banks and answer
two things about each one.

There is **no score for you** here. A low number is not a bad
performance — it is information about the questions, which is exactly
what we are measuring. Answering honestly is worth far more than
answering well.

Please **do not look at any existing results** before you finish, and do
not ask anyone what they answered.

## Sign in

## Start — do this twice

1. Go to **Bank QC → Review**.
2. Under **Cohort**, choose **Academic Talk**.
3. Under **Items**, choose **20**.
4. Press **Start a sitting**.

## Each item has two steps

### Step 1 — blind

You see **four options and nothing else**. No reading passage, no audio,
no question stem. The screen says *"Blind — no stimulus. Which is the
intended answer?"*

Pick the option you think was meant to be correct.

You are not being asked to know the answer — you cannot, because the
source is hidden. You are being asked whether the options **alone** give
it away. So:

- If something about the options points to one of them, pick it.
- If nothing does, press **"Can't tell — all four look equal to me"**.
  It is recorded as a real answer, not a skip.

**What "Can't tell" means here, exactly.** It does **not** mean "I am not
sure". You will never be sure — the source is hidden, so being unsure is
the normal state and is not a reason to press it. It means the narrower
thing: *nothing in these four options points anywhere.*

So if you find yourself thinking any of these, **pick the option**:

- "Three of these are obviously wrong, so it must be the fourth."
- "This one is longer / more careful / more hedged than the others."
- "This one sounds like the sort of thing a test wants."

All three are the measurement working. A hunch you cannot justify is
still a hunch the options gave you, and that is precisely what we are
counting. Press "Can't tell" only when all four options look equally
possible to you and you would be picking at random.

Both mistakes cost us the same amount, so please do neither:

- **Guessing when nothing points anywhere** makes the questions look
  worse than they are.
- **Pressing "Can't tell" when something does point at an option** makes
  them look better than they are.

Answer the way you actually read it. There is no target number, and
neither answer is the safe one.

### Step 2 — revealed

Now you see the passage, the question and the real answer. Two
judgements:

**Is the key the only defensible answer?**

| Choose | When |
|---|---|
| Only defensible answer | The marked answer is right and nothing else works |
| Another is also defensible | A second option could be argued for by a reasonable candidate |
| No unique answer / key is wrong | Two or more work equally well, or the marked answer is simply wrong |

Be strict on the middle option. "Defensible" means a careful test-taker
could argue for it, not that it is merely not silly.

**Does it read like a real exam question?**

*Authentic*, or *Authored to a template* if it feels machine-made or
formulaic.

There is a free-text note box. **Use it whenever something is off** — an
awkward phrase, an option that gives the game away, anything you would
flag if you were proofing the test. Every real defect found so far that
no automated check had caught came out of these notes, not the scores.
