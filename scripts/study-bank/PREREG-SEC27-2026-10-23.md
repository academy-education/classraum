### What the dry run changed in the table before this document
- **Boundary mark sets were searched**, not hand-picked (`sec27-build/marksearch*.mjs`, scratchpad). The
  first hand design offered the trailing comma in 11 of 12 items with 1 key, and the bare option in 12
  with 1 key: SC8 would have failed as SEC26 did. A full-balance design is arithmetically impossible
  (keyed rates must average exactly 1/4 over shapes, so every shape would need exactly n = 4k), which is
  why SC8 allows one shape to be one key short; the table uses that slack once (semicolon 1 of 7).
- **SC8 and B5 conflicted**: the bare option must be keyed in about a quarter of the 12 items that need
  it, and SEC26's B5 allowed at most 2 bare keys. B5's cap was raised to 3 (row 6), the one bar change.
- **D9** was first a comma key with a colon offered (SEC26D-09's non-exclusive set); re-keyed colon.
- **C8** (i\|n) collided with B9 on (option shape x decider type), and C7 (r\|i) with D7; re-gridded
  C8 j\|m + seldom and C7 e\|h, both found by `sec27-brief-counts.mjs` on the fixture.
- **SC7's lure rule could not see grader f's pronoun tell** on SEC26's table (4/8 by near-noun number,
  a pass); "the key's antecedent is never the nearest noun" was added (SEC26 table 8/8, a miss).
- **Letters, near/first/after** were searched (`sec27-build/build.mjs`) against `sec27-tellsim.mjs`
  itself, with the Key x Near cells held at 4-10. The searched order has aligned quads 3/10, at the bar.

## Bars (SEC26's, unchanged) and the measurable range of each
- **Blind (batch level):** candidate minus matched control <= **+10** points (rounded to 0.1); above that
  the batch is HELD whole. Ranges as stage 2.
- **Unanimity** is a batch-level rate against the control's, never a per-item drop on its own. The three
  solvers are one model sampled three times: margins stand, intervals are about sqrt(3) wider than
  printed. An options-only pass is reported as "the option set alone does not decide it", never "the batch
  does not leak".
- **Per-item drop** (`sec27-gate.mjs`): 1 key disputed by any grader, or a cold miss (Conventions also
  needs 3/3 key votes at insert); 2 not exclusive by majority; 3 distractors weak by majority; 4 median
  n_struck >= 2; 5 path wrong per any grader; 6 a majority resolving word within four words after the
  blank; 7 a majority `matches_open_mark: true`; R any recommend_drop; E panel-median easy.
- **Elimination (A23):** as stage 4.
- **Difficulty:** the panel MEDIAN, never the author's label and never the probe's.
- **Conservative extensions:** an explicit recommend_drop drops; majority off-blueprint is HELD for Andy;
  a frozen item still flagged by `sec27-exclusion.mjs` is HELD.
- **Template pair, minimum cover with a fixed tie-break (v22's rule):** edges are pairs named by 2+ of 3
  graders in `summary.near_duplicates` in the phase-1 snapshot, both endpoints otherwise kept. Hold set:

## What the method still cannot fix
- The semantic check sees nine named rules. SEC26's readers named five; a sixth kind will be named by the
  next readers, and the check will not have seen it. Its job is to stop the KNOWN templates from being
  rebuilt; the probe is what finds the next one.
- The semicolon is the one shape keyed below a quarter (1 of 7) — the SC8 slack, used once. A reader may
  name "the semicolon rarely wins"; if so, it is recorded against the table, not the authors.
- Families owned by one author mean one author's habits can make a family read as one template; the
  brief asks for distinct skeletons inside each family, and the cover still holds k-1 of any named group.
- Four ellipsis items are the batch's best hard prospect and its biggest cover exposure if graders call
  cataphoric and anaphoric ellipsis one template.
- Tense and aspect deciders that must be inferred are harder to write exclusively; the risk moves from
  "too easy" to "non-exclusive" (a grader who reads an inferred past as possibly ongoing). Rule 2 drops
  those.
- The declarations are wording discipline; an author declares what it already believes, which is why the
  semantic check also reads the text and takes the larger count.
- Two probe readers and three solvers are samples of one model. The gate is model-only; the human sitting
  decides verbal cohorts.
