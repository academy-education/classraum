## What this was for, and what it decided

cr-v4 scored +22.2, the only encouraging number in five attempts, and I
argued once that it had never been re-tested unchanged: cr-v5 differed
from it by a ban rule PLUS my own authoring drift, and the drift was
measurably what failed. That argument was the sole remaining case for
continuing, and Andy chose to spend one attempt on it.

**It is refuted.** The same brief, held exactly, with nothing added and
nothing removed, on fresh scenarios, produced +47.2 — indistinguishable
from cr-v5 and from the original defect.

So cr-v4's +22.2 was a draw, not a method. The pre-registration
anticipated precisely this and called 20-30 at n=12 *inconclusive*
rather than a pass; that caution turns out to have been the whole
difference between reading the evidence correctly and spending three
more rounds on a number that was never there.

## The honest read of all five attempts

Nothing has moved the margin. The one apparent exception did not
replicate. Whatever produces this tell survives:

- rewriting the defective distractors (cr-v1 → repair)
- replacing the distractor method entirely (→ cr-v3)
- varying the stimulus (→ cr-v4, apparently, then not)
- banning a specific construction (→ cr-v5)
- holding the best brief exactly (→ cr-v6)

Every solver in every round describes the same thing in its own words:
the key is the option that takes up the news and does something about
it, and the distractors are the alternative states of the world. That is
not a property of any brief I wrote. It may simply be what a
four-option reply item IS when the stimulus is removed — in which case
the task type cannot be fixed by authoring, only by not weighting it
heavily.
