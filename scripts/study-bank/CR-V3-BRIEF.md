# Choose a Response — authoring brief v3

**Supersedes cr-v1 (the broken spec) and cr-v2.** Written to the
pre-registration in `A3-STAGE1-PREREGISTERED.md` §3.

---

## The one rule

> **Every option must be a natural, competent reply to *some* plausible
> spoken line. A distractor is wrong because it answers a DIFFERENT
> line — never because of how it is said.**

A distractor is not rude, not over-formal, not dismissive, not
off-topic. Those are the four slots of the cr-v1 roster, and all three
blind solvers named them unprompted. When the categories are in the
spec, the categories become the answer: the key is simply the option
that is none of them, and the listener never has to hear the line.

## How to build one

Write the spoken line first. Then write the **three near-miss lines** it
could have been — one fact changed in each — and write the natural reply
to each of those. Those replies are your distractors.

    spoken line   "The grant covers travel but not accommodation, and the
                   conference hotel is the only one within an hour."

    near-miss A   covers accommodation but not travel
    near-miss B   covers both
    near-miss C   there are other hotels nearby

Each distractor is now the *correct* answer to a question that was not
asked. Read alone, all four are competent, cooperative and
indistinguishable. The only way to choose is to have heard which line
was actually spoken.

## Three constraints, from the pre-registration

1. **Never specify a roster of distractor types.** Including a new one.
   The near-miss method generates distractors from the CONTENT of each
   item, so no two items share a distractor taxonomy.

2. **No option may be rejectable without the prompt.** If a reader can
   throw an option out by reading it in isolation — internally
   contradictory, absurd on its face, obviously impolite — it is not a
   distractor. One of the 24 replacements written on 2026-08-06 was
   self-contradictory and solvers cracked it on that alone.

3. **Vary the load-bearing element across items.** Do not let the key
   always accept, always ask a question, or always be the reply that
   "engages with the constraint". Let it sometimes decline, sometimes
   correct a premise, sometimes propose a compromise. Per the CLAUDE.md
   corollary: the more rigid the spec, the more the answer is
   predictable from the spec rather than the content.

## Mechanical

- Key letters flat by construction (3 each of A/B/C/D per 12), so the
  control is honest and no letter-spread check is doing work here.
- Register: neutral academic-adjacent English, contractions allowed,
  the way people actually speak.
- Explanations name each option by its CONTENT, never by position —
  options are reshuffled at draw time (see migration 078 and A13).

## What kills a batch authored to this brief

Recorded in advance, because every previous round found its tell after
banking:

- If the key is reliably the **longest** option, or the only one with a
  question mark, or the only one naming a specific detail from the line,
  the near-miss method has been applied lazily. Distractors must draw on
  the same specificity, because they answer a real line too.
- If a solver can score by picking "the reply that mentions a
  consequence", the near-misses were too far from the line. Pull them
  closer.
