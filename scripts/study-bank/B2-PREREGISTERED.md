# B2 — decision rule, fixed before the data exists

**Written 2026-08-09. No B2 sitting has been taken.** That is the whole
point: B1's pre-registration was drafted after reviewer 1 had sat, and it
came out void. A rule written around a number it has already seen is not
a rule.

---

## 1. The question

Two cohorts read as near-perfectly guessable to a model shown only the
four options:

| cohort | items | blind |
|---|---|---|
| Academic Talk (TOEFL) | 275 | 100% |
| Craft and Structure (SAT) | 211 | 97.4% |

Does a person, given the same four options and nothing else, do the same?

## 2. What we already know about the instrument

Every cohort a human has actually read came back far below the model:

| cohort | blind | by hand |
|---|---|---|
| Announcement | 100% | **15.0%** (n=20) |
| Daily Life | 100% | **21.4%** (n=14) |
| Academic Passage | 100% | **41.7%** (n=12) |

Three for three, the model over-called. Chance on four options is 25%,
so two of those three sit at or below chance. The live hypothesis is
that the blind attack is a **screen**, not a verdict.

## 3. The rule

One reader, 20 items per cohort. Score = correct ÷ **items shown**,
counting "Can't tell" as not-correct. Each cohort is judged on its own.

| human score | verdict |
|---|---|
| **≤ 35%** | **CLEARED** — consistent with chance; the model over-called |
| **36–59%** | **INCONCLUSIVE** — a second reader decides, nothing else changes |
| **≥ 60%** | **CONFIRMED guessable** — the cohort needs work |

35% is chance (25%) plus one standard error at n=20 (~10 points). 60% is
where a reader is right more than twice as often as chance. The gap
between them is wide **on purpose**: at n=20 a single item moves the
score 5 points, and a rule with no dead zone would let one item decide
486 items' fate.

## 4. Committed in advance — the parts that will be tempting to bend

- **The threshold does not move after the number arrives.** If a cohort
  lands at 36%, it is inconclusive, not "basically cleared".
- **Abstention rate is reported, never used to adjust the verdict.**
  B1's second sitting produced 17 "Can't tell" out of 20 and that got
  read as caution; it is equally consistent with a brief that pushed
  abstention. Record it, do not interpret it.
- **The notes do not override the score.** Defects found in the notes
  are logged as their own findings. A reviewer writing "these felt
  obvious" while scoring 20% does not make the cohort broken.
- **Neither cohort's result decides the other**, and neither decides the
  seven other unconfirmed cohorts. A clear on Academic Talk is evidence
  about the *instrument*, not a clearance for 1,764 items nobody has
  read. Any such clearance requires its own sitting.
- **n=20 is one reader.** Every verdict here is provisional in the same
  way Announcement's is, and should be labelled that way in the register.

## 5. What each outcome changes

**Both cleared** → the blind attack is downgraded to a screen bank-wide,
and its margins stop being quoted as findings on their own. A3's +45.1
gets re-read: Choose a Response still has a *human* number agreeing with
it (53.3%), so it does not clear automatically — but the case for
authoring ~200 replacement items weakens, and the first move becomes
re-measuring rather than rebuilding.

**Both confirmed** → 486 items are genuinely guessable, the problem is
roughly 7× A3, and A3 stops being the priority. The next question is
whether it is one authoring brief or several, which is a different
investigation.

**Split** → the most informative outcome, and the one to hope for. TOEFL
Listening and SAT Reading were authored by different pipelines; a split
localises the defect to a pipeline rather than to the bank.

## 6. What this cannot tell us

Whether these items are *good*. A cohort that is not guessable from the
options can still be badly written, mis-keyed, or unlike the real exam.
The step-2 judgements and the notes speak to that, and they are recorded
separately from the blind score for exactly that reason.
