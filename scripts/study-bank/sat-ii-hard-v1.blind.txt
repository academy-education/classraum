### Item II-H-01  (Information and Ideas / Central Ideas and Details)
Passage: Excavators at a Neolithic settlement found large grain pits at the village center, which some researchers read as evidence of collective storage and shared risk. But the pits' contents differ sharply: two hold barley of a single, carefully sorted variety, while the rest hold mixed and partly spoiled grain. The two sorted pits also sit within the walls of the largest houses, whose floors were replastered far more often than others. The village may therefore have pooled part of its harvest while allowing certain households to keep, and to display, a better stock.
Question: Which choice best states the main idea of the text?
  (A) Signs of collective storage at the settlement postdate a period in which individual households sorted and stored their own barley.
  (B) The central pits point to shared storage, but the sorted barley held inside the largest houses indicates that grain quality was not evenly distributed.
  (C) The central pits point to shared storage, but the spoiled grain they contain shows that the practice had been abandoned before the largest houses were built.
  (D) The frequent replastering of the largest houses' floors suggests that those houses, rather than the central pits, served as the settlement's principal storage area.
