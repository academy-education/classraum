## What is being built

24 four-option MC items, all original, all in batch files only:

- `map-pilot-2-rlu.batch.json`: 12 grades 5-7 Reading + Language Usage,
  targeted at RIT 180-209. Author A (fresh Claude agent). Fixed mix:
  - **6 comprehension** (theme, summary, central idea, literary point of view,
    informational purpose, structure). Batch 1 had 5; one more because this is
    the stratum the batch exists to fix, and n=5 was too thin to read.
  - **3 vocabulary in context**.
  - **3 usage**: 2 grammar (each varies ONE dimension, declared in a
    `varied_dimension` field) and 1 writing (transition or precise word).
- `map-pilot-2-mech.batch.json`: 6 Capitalization + 6 Spelling across target
  bands 180-239. Author B (fresh Claude agent).

**Source policy, unchanged (MAP-RESEARCH §8, §9).** Authors and graders work
only from NWEA's public CCSS_2024 strand names (MAP-RESEARCH §12) and general
CCSS ELA grade 5-7 standards. No NWEA item, live or reconstructed, is opened,
including the Herald / cloud.dherald.com PDFs and the batch-1 files are not
shown to the authors (so they cannot style-match a failed batch). Claude
subagents only; at most two run at once.

**Disposition, fixed now: NOT INSERTED.** Same reason as batch 1: MAP has no
shipped family, inserter, ledger family, topic or blueprint, and a staged row
is still a row. No shipped-test, topic or blueprint file is touched.

## What the authors are told (from batch-1 results §"What a second batch should change")

1. **Mechanics: break the centroid.** Distractors must differ from each
   other, not only from the key. The recommended construction is the 2x2:
   two error sites, options = {neither, first only, second only, both}, key =
   neither. Any construction that passes E7 is allowed.
2. **No dead distractors.** Every wrong option is a mistake a grade 5-7
   student plausibly makes; no triple letters, no lowercased names, no
   randomly capitalized common nouns, no options that are off-topic.
3. **Comprehension options all make the same KIND of claim**, passage-specific,
   same scope, same hedge level; no generic moral beside a specific one, no
   absolutes, no narrow detail beside central ideas, no summary that invents
   events. The test the author applies: *with the passage covered, are all four
   still possible?* (CLAUDE.md: an item leaks when the options differ along
   the axis the stem names.)
4. **Grammar varies one dimension** (batch-1 RLU-12 varied tense and agreement).
5. **Author one band harder than instinct.** Batch-1 graders called 17/48
   judgements easier than target and 0 harder. Spelling vocabulary floored at
   grade level (no grade 1-3 sight words labelled grade 5+).
6. Key letters: author's slot choice is irrelevant (every render reshuffles),
   but E8 checks it anyway.

## Exact checks (script: `map-pilot-2-checks.mjs`, which imports batch 1's
break-tested `map-pilot-checks.mjs` for E1-E6 unchanged)

E1-E6 exactly as batch 1 (FK [4.0, 9.0) with the <15-word "unscorable" rule;
key length <= 25% uniquely longest/shortest; NSSpellChecker + web2 spelling;
case-only capitalization with distinct `cap_rule`; width 4). Two new gates,
both decidable and so checked over the whole population:

- **E7 centroid (medoid) gate, NEW, per item.** For every Capitalization item
  and every correct-polarity Spelling item: compute each option's summed
  distance to the other three (case-position Hamming for capitalization,
  Damerau-Levenshtein for spelling). **No option may be the UNIQUE minimum.**
  This bans the batch-1 tell (key = unique consensus) AND its inversion
  (a distractor as the unique consensus would teach "never pick the centre").
  Batch 1's majority-vote P2 metric is also printed for comparison (batch 1:
  10 of 11). Break-test: batch 1's own Mechanics file must fail E7 on at least
  8 items, and a 2x2 item must pass.
  *Change from batch 1, justified by batch-1 results finding 1 (an exact,
  whole-population defect named unprompted by a grader).*
- **E8 key periodicity, NEW.** On every key-letter sequence that a reader
  sees in order (each authored file, the with-source render, the blind
  render): for lags 1-6, the rate of `key[i] == key[i+lag]` must stay below
  50% (chance 25%); no window of 8 consecutive keys may be exactly periodic
  at lag 2, 3 or 4; and no letter may run 3+ in a row. Batch 1's R/LU file
  and its with-source render both open B,D,A,C,B,D,A,C (a lag-4 cycle: 75%
  at lag 4 over the R/LU file, diluted to 30% over the 24-item render, which
  is why the window rule exists). Break-test: that sequence and round-robin
  ABCD must fail; a seeded random flat sequence must pass the lag and window
  rules. (Runs of 3 occur by chance in about half of random 24-letter
  sequences, so the run rule is met by choosing the deal, and authors are
  told to avoid runs.) The blind render is
  dealt flat per arm in a seeded random order (not `i % 4` round-robin, which
  is a period-4 cycle by construction).
  *New because the owner asked for it and batch 1's with-source render had it.*

One pre-attack repair per failing item, else drop. After the attack, drops only.

## Options-only screen (a SCREEN by stratum; never a verdict)

**Render.** One file, options only, stems and passages withheld, items
interleaved by seed, keys dealt flat **per arm** in a non-periodic order.
Arms:

| arm | n | role |
|---|---|---|
| comprehension (candidate) | 6 | the stratum to fix |
| vocabulary (candidate) | 3 | |
| usage (candidate) | 3 | |
| mechanics (candidate) | 12 | decides nothing |
| control V: the SAME 12 ISEE easy verbal items as batch 1 (`map-pilot-control.batch.json`) | 12 | instrument sanity; comparability with batch 1 |
| control R: 8 live ISEE Reading Comprehension `main-idea` easy items, drawn by seed | 8 | **NEW: comprehension-shaped control.** Batch 1's only control was vocabulary-shaped and matched the stratum that came in clean, not the one that leaked |

Each arm is scored against its OWN realised best-fixed-letter rate.

**Solvers.** Three samples of one Claude solver (three samples, not three
solvers). Each opens only the blind file.

**Bars:**

- **Comprehension (decides whether batch 1's leak was fixed, as a screen).**
  Batch 1: 15/15 (100%), unanimous on key 5/5.
  - **FIXED** if mean <= 50% AND unanimous-on-key <= 2 of 6.
  - **NOT FIXED** if mean >= 75% OR unanimous-on-key >= 4 of 6.
  - Between: inconclusive, reported as such.
  - Control R is reported beside it. **Ceiling clause:** if control R comes in
    at or above 75%, the instrument saturates on this item type (as CLAUDE.md
    records for SAT C&S and ACT Reading) and the comprehension number is
    reported as "candidate vs saturated control", never as a leak verdict on
    its own; the absolute FIXED / NOT FIXED call above still stands because it
    is a comparison with batch 1 through the identical instrument.
- **Vocabulary, usage:** reported against batch 1 (0/6, 3/9) and control V. n=3
  each is noise; decides nothing alone.
- **Mechanics:** reported, decides nothing (the option set is the item).
- Unanimity is reported per arm against the controls' rate and never drops an
  item alone (CLAUDE.md 2026-09-25).

## With-source graders (two fresh Claude graders, all 24, key unmarked)

Each returns per item: `pick`, `second_defensible`, `grade_fit`
(fits / too_easy / too_hard + note), **`band_assigned`** (the ten-point RIT
band, e.g. "RIT 200-209", the grader would target it at; NEW), `band`
(plausible / easier / harder relative to the stated target),
`free_elimination` (any option killable with no reading or work, or null),
**`dead_distractors`** (letters no grade 5-7 student would plausibly pick;
NEW), and for grammar items **`dimensions_varied`** (NEW).

**Per item, pilot-pass requires (batch 1's three, unchanged):**
1. both graders pick the key;
2. neither names a second defensible answer;
3. neither rates `grade_fit` off;

**plus one new condition:**
4. no option is named a dead distractor by BOTH graders.
*Added because batch-1 results tied every band miss to dead distractors. The
batch-1-comparable count (conditions 1-3 only) is ALSO reported, so the
comparison with batch 1's 18/24 is like-for-like.*

**Batch bars:**
- **The method works** if at least 20 of 24 reach pilot-pass (all four
  conditions) and every exact check (E1-E8) passes. Same 20 as batch 1.
- **Band targeting unsupported** if both graders call >= 50% `easier` or
  `harder` (unchanged).
- **Calibration (NEW, secondary, reported):** batch 1 had 17 of 48 judgements
  `easier`, 0 `harder`. Batch 2 calibration IMPROVED if `easier` <= 8 of 48;
  OVERSHOT if `harder` >= 12 of 48. `band_assigned` is compared to the target
  by script (difference in ten-point bands), not read off the grader's word.
- **Comprehension free eliminations (NEW, secondary):** batch 1 had a free
  elimination named by both graders on 5/5. IMPROVED if both graders name one
  on <= 2 of 6.

Pilot-pass still means "fit to show a human". It is not "shippable", and no
RIT is attached to any item.

## What can't be learned here, stated in advance

- Whether any band label is right (MAP-RESEARCH §10): graders reading norms
  are not response data.
- Whether students find the items hard: a human sitting decides verbal
  cohorts, and MAP has none.
- Whether the 2x2 Mechanics construction carries its own tell. It defeats the
  centroid by construction; a tell it introduces would have to be found by the
  graders or a human.
