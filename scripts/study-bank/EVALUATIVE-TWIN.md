# The evaluative twin — an authoring rule for bijective sets

Found 2026-08-31 by the exclusivity reviewer on ssat-verbal-s5, from the
pattern in its own failures. Five of fifteen synonym topics failed and
four failed the same way.

## The pattern

Two words describe substantially the SAME behaviour and differ mainly in
APPROVAL:

    foolhardy / intrepid      bold — disapproved / approved
    magnanimous / benevolent  big-hearted, both ways
    bulky / colossal          large — encumbering / impressive
    scant / minuscule         small — insufficient / merely little
    balk / defy               refusal — passive / confrontational

In a bijective set this is fatal, and the reason is precise: the two
glosses describe the same act, and the charge lives in CONNOTATION,
which a gloss cannot carry. So the off-diagonal option stays TRUE of the
stem even though it is the worse answer — and an item with a true wrong
answer has two correct answers.

## The rule that follows, and it is not the obvious one

**Narrowing an answer protects only the direction you narrowed.**

The author saw the magnanimous/benevolent collision and narrowed
MAGNANIMOUS's own gloss to "generous in forgiving a rival". That is a
real sense and it does stop that gloss fitting BENEVOLENT. It does
nothing to stop BENEVOLENT's gloss — "kindly and wishing others well" —
from fitting the MAGNANIMOUS stem, because magnanimous also just means
big-hearted. The leak ran the other way and the repair never touched it.

So: **check both directions of every near-pair, and repair by REPLACING
one member, not by re-wording a gloss.**

## Where these sets are safest

The reviewer's own observation, worth keeping:

  - verb sets with distinct DIRECTIONS of motion or change pass cleanest
  - sets spread over an axis with opposite poles pass
  - failures cluster where three or more members sit on the SAME SIDE of
    one magnitude or valence axis — size, generosity, courage

That is a pre-flight question an author can ask without a reviewer: are
my five words all "more X" or "less X" of one thing? If so, expect a
twin.

## A second, smaller pattern

The person/thing boundary does silent work. SPURIOUS is normally said of
things and DUPLICITOUS of people, and SUBVERT names a mechanism THWART
does not — but a gloss that omits the restriction does not enforce it.
If the distinction between two answers is a selectional restriction,
state it IN the gloss or the set is not bijective.

## What this does not mean

The bijective method itself is not implicated. Ten of fifteen topics
passed with all twenty cells clean, and the reviewer DISAGREED with the
author's doubt on one of them (ambiguous/convoluted: meaning-plurality
versus structural complexity, neither entailing the other). The method
works; choosing five mutually exclusive words is simply harder than it
looks, and this is the failure mode to design against.
