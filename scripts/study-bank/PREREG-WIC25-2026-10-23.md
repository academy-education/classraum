## Why a new method, and why it is not a third rewrite of the same brief
Two agent WIC batches failed stage 2 by a modest margin:

| Batch | E | Bar |
|---|---|---|
| v23 | +17.0 | +10 |
| v24 | +12.3 | +10 |

From the four bare options, the solvers named the key with three tricks:
- the most natural or most precise word ("most common academic sense" 12/19 correct on v23);
- one side of an opposite pair;
- the odd one out.

v24's own result note named the leak no rule targeted: the key was the most natural collocation. Every rule so far
constrained the words AROUND a key the author had already chosen, so the provenance stayed.

The owner's directive is that hard SAT WIC items come from agents. v24's result noted that the register's
agent-rewrite rule advises against a third brief of the same kind. This round is a different construction, not a
rewrite of v24's rules. It is the precedent that fixed this exact leak shape in TOEFL Choose a Response:
- `render-crv7.mjs`;
- `crv7-b14.RELEASE.PREREG.md`, where four-world batches 12, 13 and 14 were flagged 25%, 15% and 10%.

## The method
Each item has ONE option set of four words and FOUR passage variants ("worlds"). In world k, option k is the unique
key, and the other three are killed by that passage's logic.

Every world contains the same **blank sentence**, verbatim. The worlds differ only in their deciding sentences
(checked: >= 40% shared content words between every pair).

After freeze, a seeded draw picks ONE world per item. That world becomes the served item; the other three are never
shown to any reader, grader or student. Every option is the key of some world, and the draw cannot see the text, so
**the option set alone carries no information about which option is keyed.** The expected options-only margin is 0
by construction, not by authoring discipline.

The same construction also delivers v24's hardness rule. The blank sentence is identical across four worlds that
have four different keys, so it can never decide the key by itself. Every kill must also quote text outside the
blank sentence (checked). v24's deletion test is therefore satisfied structurally, not by the author's label.

### Selection rule, fixed now
`wic25-select.mjs`:
1. Refuse unless `wic25-selfcheck.mjs --final` is green and each author has exactly 8 files.
2. Write `sat-cs-wic-v25.worlds.json`: all items, all four worlds, ids sorted. This is the frozen text, and its
   sha256 is recorded.
3. Compute the selection seed: **20262590 + int(first 8 hex digits of that sha256), mod 2^32.** mulberry32
   (`seeded-shuffle.mjs`) draws world index floor(4u) per item, in id order.
   - The base and the rule are committed here.
   - WHICH world is served cannot be computed until the text is frozen, so neither an author nor a steering probe
     can put effort into the served world.
   - crv7 used a fixed seed. This is stricter.
4. **No re-roll.** I first copied crv7's quota re-roll: re-draw if the served key is the rarest, most common,
   longest or shortest option on > 35% of items. The break-test below REJECTED it. Over 4,000 base seeds it tilted
   served keys toward the middle rarity rank, chi2 3,222 against 0.5 without it. That would rebuild a "pick the
   middle word" tell. Any acceptance rule that reads the served key's features conditions the key on them. The
   realised ranks are reported only, and the stage-5 tells decide.
5. Deal letters from a balanced deck (8 per letter), seeded rng(seed + 7919), a separate stream.
6. Write two files:
   - `sat-cs-wic-v25.batch.json`: served world only, in v24's field set;
   - `sat-cs-wic-v25.selection.json`: worlds sha, seed, and world index per item. Never shown to anyone in the
     pipeline.
7. **No post-selection edits.** A served world that fails any gate drops its item WHOLE. An item is never re-served
   from another of its worlds; that would be selection on quality.

### Per-item rules (self-check)
- Four worlds whose keys are the four choices, each used once.
- `blank_sentence` verbatim in every world, and unique across items.
- Each world:
  - 50-110 words;
  - one six-underscore blank;
  - no a/an before the blank;
  - `after_blank` matches;
  - `key_from_one_sentence: false`;
  - an explanation quoting the key and each wrong word;
  - a kill for each wrong word, quoting 2+ words of that world outside the blank sentence;
  - at most one `explicit` kill.
- World lengths within 1.3x of each other.
- `antonym_pairs: []`.
- Words:
  - lowercase and word-shaped, one shape per item;
  - in range;
  - not denylisted, and not the brief's worked-example words;
  - not a live, v17-v24 frozen-file word, stem or inflection (v19/v20/v23/v24 are read from the frozen files as
    well, so the net does not depend on the live-words file);
  - no stem twice in the batch.

### Per-author rules
- 8 items.
- >= 3 noun/verb option sets.
- >= 4 distinct world shapes.
- "Received view overturned" in <= 25% of worlds.

### Dropped from v24, and why
These rules cannot carry signal when every option is a key:
- rarity count rules (key rarest <= 2, commonest >= 2);
- key-not-odd-one-out in polarity and register;
- the per-author key-length band.

`rarity_order` is still declared; it feeds the stage-5 report.

### Pre-freeze steering (decides nothing at the gate)
v24's options-only r1/r2 probes measure nothing here, because all four words are keys while authoring. They are
replaced by the **world probe**, `wic25-wprobe.mjs`:
- **Four renders.** Render r holds world (r + i) mod 4 of item i. Each render shows every item exactly once, so no
  reader sees two worlds of one item and can eliminate across them. Each render goes to a fresh reader
  (`WPROBE-BRIEF.md`). Seed 20262583.
- **Naming.** A world is named if:
  - its key is not recovered; or
  - it is rated easy; or
  - it is marked key-from-one-sentence; or
  - it has >= 2 explicit kills; or
  - it has a second defensible option.
- **ONE revision round** of the named worlds, then freeze.

Because the selection seed depends on the frozen sha, this steering cannot favour the served worlds.

## Stages and bars (v24's, unchanged except where stated)
The run stops at the first failed stage.

### Stage 1: structure and duplicates
bank-helper structural check OK on `sat-cs-wic-v25.batch.json`, and dupscan against the live bank.

### Stage 2: options-only attack (`wic25-oo-render.mjs`, seed 20262591)
Three samples of one solver prompt (`OO-SOLVER-BRIEF.md`): three samples, not three solvers. Scored by `score-oo.mjs`.

- **Control:** the WHOLE eligible word-shaped live WIC pool, hard + medium. The dry render counted 100. The
  render refuses the worlds file.
- **Measure:** M = arm rate minus the arm's own best-fixed-letter line. **PASS if E = M_cand - M_ctl <= +10.**
- **Attainable range:**
  - The candidate line is 25.0% (8/8/8/8), so M_cand spans [-25, +75].
  - The control is measured only inside (line + 5, 90%] = (30%, 90%]. v23 read 36.3% and v24 34.3%.
  - With the control anywhere in that range, E can land on either side of +10, so the bar can fire both ways.
  - If the control is outside the range, stage 2 is NOT MEASURED, not passed. The batch then continues only if
    M_cand <= +10 on its own.
- **What a pass means here.** The candidate is expected at chance, E about -10. **Stage 2 is now a check that
  selection and rendering did not leak, not evidence about item quality.** It fails if:
  - the selection was not independent of the text;
  - an unserved world reached a reader;
  - something other than the option set carries the key.

  Never report "options-only clean" as "the batch does not leak". With the source, the four-world construction can
  leak in its own ways (see the limits below).
- **Unanimity** is a batch-level comparison against the control's rate, never a per-item verdict.

### Stage 3: options-only elimination
Command: `elimination-paired.mjs render rw-v25-cs-wic --domain "Craft and Structure" --tag wic25-elim --batch
sat-cs-wic-v25.batch.json --match subskill --ratio 1`

Three samples per arm. Margin <= +20, control <= 80%, n >= 12. This is also expected near 0 by construction.

### Stage 4: with-source graders (`wic25-gate.mjs`)
- **Render.** `BLIND=bare bank-helper.mjs blind sat-cs-wic-v25.batch.json`, the served worlds only.
- **Graders.** Three fresh Claude graders (`GRADER-BRIEF.md`), never an author, probe reader or solver. They are not
  told about worlds.
- **Phase 1** is cold. Its files are sha-recorded (`wic25.ws-phase1.sha256`) before phase 2 shows the key.
- **Stage bar:** any grader who cold-misses more than 3 of 32 (10%) fails the whole batch.
- **Per item: DROP** on any of:
  - gate-verdict rules 1-6;
  - key above ceiling per any grader;
  - the same distractor above ceiling per >= 2;
  - an antonym pair per >= 2;
  - recommend_drop per any;
  - panel-median easy.
- **HELD** on a majority off_blueprint, or on dup-scan.
- **Rule 8:** if a majority marks key-from-one-sentence, the banked difficulty is capped at medium.
- A dropped item is dropped whole (see the selection rule).

### Stage 5: tells on the kept set
- `verify-answer-key-spread.ts --batch`: no FAIL.
- Key strictly longest <= 35%, and key strictly shortest <= 35%.
- No repeated option words.
- **NEW, the attrition check.** `wic25-gate.mjs` re-scores the stage-2 picks on the KEPT candidates only:
  **E_kept = M_kept - M_ctl <= +10**, with M_kept against the kept arm's own letter line.
  - **Why it is needed.** A world keyed on an awkward word is harder to make exclusive, so graders drop it more
    often. The kept keys can then drift back toward the natural word. That would rebuild the very leak the method
    removed, invisibly to the whole-batch stage 2.
  - **Fewer than 8 kept:** not measured. The kept set is HELD for the owner, not passed and not discarded. A human
    sitting is the decisive instrument anyway.
  - The kept keys' declared rarity ranks (1-4) are printed, but they decide nothing.

## What the method cannot fix
- **Difficulty and exclusivity per world** are still the author's to get right, and only graders can judge them. The
  self-check verifies structure (identical blank sentence, kills quoted from outside it), not that a kill actually
  works. Four worlds per item means four chances to write a second defensible answer.
- **Only the served world is graded.** The other 96 worlds are checked only by the steering probe, which decides
  nothing. That is acceptable only because they are never served. **Unserved worlds must never be banked or
  recycled**: re-serving an item from another world would put the same option set in front of students with a
  different key. The words stay excluded for later rounds.
- **With-source tells specific to this construction:**
  - one "native" world and three contorted ones (mitigated by the 1.3x length cap and the overlap floor; judged by
    graders);
  - a deciding sentence that reads as planted.

  The options-only attack cannot see either. Stages 3 and 4 have to.
- **Attrition** (above): measured on the kept set, and not measurable below 8 kept.
- **A model-only gate.** Per CLAUDE.md, three samples are one solver sampled three times. The human sitting decides
  verbal cohorts.
