## What is built

### Changes against batch 3, each aimed at one of its two failures

**Failure 1, dead distractors (5 of 6 holds were condition 4 alone).**
1. The vocabulary rule "every option is a genuine meaning of the target word"
   is **dropped**. Options are now real words of the same part of speech that
   fit the sentence's surface (grammatical and plausible in the situation)
   but not its meaning.
2. The 2x2 "both errors" cell must be pickable (two sites the same student
   gets wrong together); transitions and grammar options must each read
   naturally locally.
3. **Teacher-voice gate before the graders (new).** One fresh Claude agent in
   a grade 5-7 teacher's voice rates each of the 72 distractors "would a real
   student pick this?" (key marked; prompt 2). Every option rated **no** goes
   to one fresh reviser (prompt 3) for a rewrite; the rewritten options are
   re-rated once by a fresh teacher agent (same prompt). **One rewrite per
   option**: an option still rated no goes to the gate as it stands and is
   reported. No item is dropped at this stage, so the population stays 24.
   *The teacher reviewer is not a grader and never sees the graders' schema;
   the graders are fresh and decide the bars.*

**Failure 2, calibration (`easier` 11/48, concentrated at 180-189).**
4. Each band is anchored to its own grade's standard (`ccss` field:
   L.5/W.5 at 180-189, L.6/W.6 at 190-199, L.7/W.7 at 200-209); no grade-5
   rule may be labelled 190+; within that standard the author writes the
   harder instance, one band harder than instinct (the brief lists grade-5
   hard-end rules for the lowest band). Graders state `band_assigned` (as in
   batch 3).
5. Vocabulary contexts must decide meaning by inference, not definition, and
   no context clue may match only the key.

`ccss` is author-declared metadata; it is reported (count of items whose
`ccss` grade differs from the band's grade) and is never treated as evidence
of difficulty.

## Order of work

author -> teacher rating -> one rewrite of "no" options -> re-rate ->
exact checks E1-E9 (one pre-attack repair per failing item, else drop) ->

options-only samples and two with-source graders -> score
(`map-pilot-4-score.mjs`). No repair round after any attack: drops only.

## Bars (passes only if ALL hold) — identical to batch 3 strand 1

| bar | threshold | batch 3 |
|---|---|---|
| S1-a exact | E1-E9 pass | pass |
| S1-b pilot-pass | **>= 20 of 24** (conditions 1-4) | 18/24 FAIL |
| S1-c calibration | `easier` **<= 8 of 48** AND `harder` **< 12 of 48** | 11 / 2 FAIL |
| S1-d band | each grader's mean (`band_assigned` - target) within **[-0.5, +0.5]** | -0.29 / -0.13 pass |

*Range checks (unchanged):* S1-b spans 0-24; S1-c's counts span 0-48 each and
both bars sit inside; S1-d is attainable from both sides. *Overshoot is
guarded*: "one band harder" can fail S1-c through `harder` >= 12 or S1-d
above +0.5, so the calibration fix cannot pass by pushing items up blindly.
`map-pilot-4-score.mjs --selftest` break-tests every bar at its margin.

**Reported, not deciding:** the batch-1-comparable count (conditions 1-3;
batch 3 23/24); condition-4 holds (batch 3: 5 on c4 alone); survivors by band
(batch 3: 4/8, 6/8, 8/8); teacher NO count before and after rewrite;
options-only arms vocabulary (n=6), usage (n=8) and mechanics (n=10, the
option set is the item) against their own best-fixed-letter lines and
**control R (the same 8 live ISEE main-idea items) and control V (the same 12
ISEE easy verbal items)**, three samples of one Claude solver (prompt 4),
unanimity as a rate against the controls' (CLAUDE.md 2026-09-25). A
vocabulary or usage arm more than 20 points above control V is flagged as a
screen finding, never as a verdict (n <= 8, correlated samples). Because the
vocabulary rule that took the arm from 55.6% to 22.2% is dropped, **a
vocabulary options-only rise is the predicted cost of fix 1** and is reported
as such; if it flags, the with-source free-elimination notes on those items
are reported beside it.

## What can't be learned here, stated in advance

- Whether any band label is right (MAP-RESEARCH §10): graders are not response
  data.
- Whether students find the items hard or pick the distractors: the teacher
  reviewer is a model in a teacher's voice, not a teacher, and a human sitting
  decides verbal cohorts. MAP has none.
- Whether the teacher gate itself helps, beyond this batch: it is applied to
  every item, so there is no within-batch comparison; the comparison is batch
  3's 5 condition-4-only holds, with a different author and brief.
