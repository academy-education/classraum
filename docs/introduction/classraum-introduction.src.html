01 — The Problem03 / 13
01

A Third of the Week Never Reaches a Student.

Educators spend 30 to 50 percent of their week on work other than teaching, spread across five to ten tools that do not share data. Little of that work is difficult, but almost all of it is repetitive.

The bar is drawn at 40 percent; the bracket marks the published range of 30 to 50. The company estimates that working across five to ten separate systems reduces meaningful student engagement by about 70 percent.

Classraum's own model for an academy of 120 students and 6 teachers assumes 18 minutes per student per month on attendance, billing and parent updates, and 4 hours per teacher per month on reports. Actual savings depend on how the academy operates.

05 — SAT Camps08 / 13
05

A Complete SAT Program, Minus the Teacher

A partnership rather than a license. The school brings the teacher, the students and the room; Classraum brings the curriculum, the tests, the grading and the analytics.

Teaching from the Data

Results are banded by the SAT's own domains, four in Math and four in Reading and Writing, so a teacher plans in the exam's categories: a low domain is re‑taught briefly, a wrong answer shared by many is corrected with the whole class, and a strong domain moves the student to harder practice.

What Counts as a User

Every account on the system: students, parents, teachers and administrators. A student with two linked parents occupies three seats, which is why user counts run well above student counts.
